CONCEPT: Rational Numbers

DEFINITION:
A rational number is a number that can be written as a fraction a divided by b, where a and b are integers and b is not zero.

EXPLANATION:
Rational numbers include all fractions, terminating decimals, and repeating decimals.
They represent exact quantities.
Rational numbers can be positive, negative, or zero.
They are commonly used in measurement, counting, and calculations.

EXAMPLES:
1/2
-3/4
3
0.5
0.75
0.333 repeating

SUMMARY:
Rational numbers are numbers that can be expressed exactly as fractions of two integers.
---

CONCEPT: Irrational Numbers

DEFINITION:
An irrational number is a number that cannot be written as a fraction of two integers.

EXPLANATION:
Irrational numbers have decimal expansions that never end and never repeat.
They cannot be expressed exactly.
They often appear in geometry and advanced mathematics.

EXAMPLES:
Square root of 2
Pi
Euler number e

SUMMARY:
Irrational numbers are non-repeating, non-terminating decimals that cannot be written as fractions.
---

CONCEPT: Real Numbers

DEFINITION:
A real number is any number that can be represented on the number line.

EXPLANATION:
Real numbers include both rational and irrational numbers.
They cover every possible point on the number line.
They are used to measure continuous values such as length and time.

EXAMPLES:
-5
0
2
1/2
Square root of 2
Pi

SUMMARY:
Real numbers include all rational and irrational numbers.
---
